Introduction

This report explores the Bank Marketing dataset which contains information about customers contacted during a direct marketing campaign conducted by a Portuguese banking institution. The primary objective is to investigate how customer characteristics and campaign interactions relate to whether a customer subscribes to a term deposit.

The response variable, y, records whether a customer subscribed to a term deposit.

The dataset was obtained from Kaggle: Banking Dataset (Marketing Targets), which is based on the UCI Bank Marketing dataset

Data Preparation

The original dataset was obtained from Kaggle and consisted of two files, ‘train.csv’ and ‘test.csv’. The datasets were combined, duplicate observations were removed, and categorical variables were converted to factors. The cleaned dataset was then saved as bank_clean.csv for use in both the Shiny application and this exploratory analysis.

Original Data Cleaning Process

The following code documents the initial data preparation procedure. It is not executed during rendering because the cleaned dataset has already been created.

Code 
library(tidyverse)

# Read both datasets
train <- read_delim("Dataset/train.csv", delim = ";")
test <- read_delim("Dataset/test.csv", delim = ";")

# Combine train and test datasets and remove duplicate rows
bank_data <- bind_rows(train, test)
bank_data <- distinct(bank_data)

# Check for remaining duplicates
sum(duplicated(bank_data))

# Clean & format
bank_data <- bank_data |>
  mutate(across(where(is.character), as.factor))

# Save combined clean data for the Shiny app
write_csv(bank_data, "Dataset/bank_clean.csv")

Discussion & Conclusion

The exploratory analysis of the Bank Marketing dataset reveals several patterns in customer demographics, financial characteristics, and term deposit subscription outcomes.

First, most customers are between approximately 25 and 60 years old, with the greatest concentration among individuals in their early to mid-30s. Account balances exhibit substantial right-skewness, with most customers having relatively low balances and a small number having exceptionally high balances. This suggests that medians may be more informative than means when describing typical account balances.

Second, subscription outcomes appear to vary across customer characteristics. Customers with tertiary education have a higher observed subscription proportion than those with primary or secondary education. Customers who subscribed also appear to have higher median account balances, although there is considerable overlap between the subscription groups.

Third, the faceted scatterplot does not indicate a strong linear relationship between age and account balance in either subscription group. The heatmap reveals additional differences across combinations of occupation and education, with students and retired customers generally displaying relatively high subscription rates. However, results for groups with small sample sizes should be interpreted with caution.

Overall, the findings suggest that education, occupation, and account balance may be associated with term deposit subscription outcomes. These results are descriptive and do not establish causal relationships. Future analyses could investigate these associations using statistical models while accounting for additional customer and campaign characteristics.

An additional consideration is that call duration is observed during or after a marketing contact. Although it may be associated with subscription outcomes, it would not necessarily be available when predicting customer responses before a call takes place.

## Discussion & Conclusion

The exploratory analysis of the Bank Marketing dataset reveals several patterns in customer demographics, financial characteristics, and term deposit subscription outcomes.

First, most customers are between approximately 25 and 60 years old, with the greatest concentration among individuals in their early to mid-30s. Account balances exhibit substantial right-skewness, with most customers having relatively low balances and a small number having exceptionally high balances. This suggests that medians may be more informative than means when describing typical account balances.

Second, subscription outcomes appear to vary across customer characteristics. Customers with tertiary education have a higher observed subscription proportion than those with primary or secondary education. Customers who subscribed also appear to have higher median account balances, although there is considerable overlap between the subscription groups.

Third, the faceted scatterplot does not indicate a strong linear relationship between age and account balance in either subscription group. The heatmap reveals additional differences across combinations of occupation and education, with students and retired customers generally displaying relatively high subscription rates. However, results for groups with small sample sizes should be interpreted with caution.

Overall, the findings suggest that education, occupation, and account balance may be associated with term deposit subscription outcomes. These results are descriptive and do not establish causal relationships. Future analyses could investigate these associations using statistical models while accounting for additional customer and campaign characteristics.

An additional consideration is that call duration is observed during or after a marketing contact. Although it may be associated with subscription outcomes, it would not necessarily be available when predicting customer responses before a call takes place.
